## The Ground Rules: Distill, Don't Copy

Nearly everything below is **free to read or watch, not free to republish**. The legally and ethically clean pipeline, which is also this knowledge base's house method:

1. **Ideas, facts, and techniques are not copyrightable** — the expression of them is. Watch the talk, understand the idea, write your own explanation, credit the source.
2. **Cite checkable provenance** — speaker, title, venue, year, and a stable URL (GDC Vault `play/ID` pages are ideal for this).
3. **Only openly licensed sources may be adapted or excerpted**, and each carries its own attribution terms (see the license table at the bottom). CC-licensed text included in an MIT repo keeps its own CC notice — it cannot be relicensed.
4. **Quote sparingly and with credit** — short attributed quotes for commentary are fair use; wholesale reproduction is not, whatever the paywall status.

## GDC's Free Ecosystem

GDC paywalls most of the Vault (~$400/yr), but gives away far more than commonly assumed:

| Source | What's There |
|--------|-------------|
| **GDC Vault free section** (gdcvault.com/free) | Free videos, audio, and slides from 1996 through the current year, filterable by track and year. Roughly 30% of recent-year content, more freed as it ages; 100+ talks released free per conference year. |
| **GDC YouTube channel** (youtube.com/@Gdconf) | ~2,000 full talks, a new one posted every weekday. The design track is heavily represented. |
| **Official slide PDFs** (media.gdcvault.com/gdcYYYY/presentations/) | GDC-published, freely downloadable decks — often available even when the video is paywalled (the free section has a "slides" filter). |
| **Game Developer magazine archive** (gdcvault.com/gdmag) | The complete 1994–2013 run as free PDFs, plus source-code zips. The magazine's Postmortem column is the origin of the entire postmortem genre. |
| **Game Developer / Gamasutra site** (gamedeveloper.com) | The written postmortem archive is free — Deus Ex (Warren Spector, 2000), Diablo II (Erich Schaefer, 2000), Thief (Tom Leonard), Age of Empires II (Matt Pritchard), and hundreds more. |

## What You Can Actually Reuse: The License Table

For an MIT-licensed project, sources split into four tiers. **Only the first tier may be adapted or excerpted**; everything else is distill-only.

### Tier 1 — Openly licensed, adaptable with attribution

| Source | License (verified) | Notes |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| Godot Engine docs | CC BY 3.0 | Attribute "Juan Linietsky, Ariel Manzur and the Godot community"; keep a CC BY notice on derived files — CC BY coexists with MIT but can't be relicensed to it |
| Ian Schreiber — *Game Design Concepts* (gamedesignconcepts.wordpress.com) | CC BY 3.0 US | A full 20-lesson game design course, explicitly adaptable — the best genuinely-open design text |
| Ian Schreiber — *Game Balance Concepts* | CC BY 3.0 US | Companion course on balance math |
| *Creating Games* open textbook (pressbooks.usnh.edu/creatinggames) | CC BY 4.0 | Itself derived from Schreiber's course — proof the adaptation chain works |
| O3DE docs | CC BY 4.0 | Linux Foundation project |
| Bevy site + book | MIT | Cleanest possible fit |
| Phaser API reference | MIT (generated from MIT source) | Tutorial site content unverified — distill-only there |
| *Game Programming Patterns* **code samples only** | MIT | The prose is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — see Tier 3 |

### Tier 2 — Open but ShareAlike (avoid adapting into an MIT repo)

Wikipedia/Wikibooks (CC BY-SA 4.0) and the libGDX wiki (CC BY-SA 4.0): adaptation is legal, but derived text must stay BY-SA forever, fragmenting the repo's licensing story. Treat as distill-only.

### Tier 3 — Free to read, NC and/or ND (no adaptation)

*Game Programming Patterns* prose (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — the author added ND deliberately), Unity Manual & Scripting Reference (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), MIT OpenCourseWare (CC BY-NC-SA), ETC Press / ToDiGRA books (CC BY-NC-ND), LearnOpenGL text (CC BY-NC).

### Tier 4 — No open license at all (default copyright)

The MDA paper (free PDF, copyright AAAI/authors), DiGRA library papers (open access, personal/educational grant only), the Chalmers gameplay design patterns wiki (live, but no license statement anywhere — all rights reserved by default), Unreal docs, all GDC Vault talks, and essentially every designer blog above. Free to learn from; distill-only.

## Applying This

- Adding to this knowledge base: distill per the ground rules; only Tier 1 sources may be adapted, each with its per-file attribution notice.
- The three richest unmined veins, by effort-to-value: the **Game Developer magazine archive** (two decades of postmortems, officially free), **Schreiber's two CC BY courses** (legally adaptable full curricula), and the **official GDC slide PDFs** (checkable primary sources even for paywalled talks).
